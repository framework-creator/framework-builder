# Competitive Analysis Framework

*This framework was generated using the Framework Builder methodology. It demonstrates the five-layer DNA architecture applied to competitive analysis for service and product businesses.*

Page: https://whereframeworks.com/frameworks/competitive-analysis
Guide: https://howtoframework.com/competitive-analysis-framework

## Layer 1: Principles Foundation

**Principle 1: Compete on depth where larger rivals compete on breadth.** Matching a bigger competitor's range is a losing game. Own one category so completely that buyers looking for it come to you first.

**Principle 2: Study a competitor's constraints, not only their capabilities.** A large firm often cannot staff small accounts with senior people because its economics forbid it. That structural limit is your opening.

**Principle 3: Track where customers are heading, not how big a rival is today.** Mid-size clients that larger rivals underserve now can be the large accounts of tomorrow.

**Principle 4: Positioning beats features.** Matching a feature removes a differentiator for both of you. Track how each rival is perceived and where it cannot credibly compete.

**Principle 5: Customers know more than competitor websites do.** The best intelligence is why buyers chose you and what almost made them go elsewhere.

## Layer 2: Systematic Approach

**Step 1: Define the competitive set in three tiers.** Direct competitors, indirect competitors (including in-house teams and freelancers) and emerging threats such as automation tools.

**Step 2: Map positioning on two axes buyers care about.** Pick the two that matter most to your target clients, for example depth of strategy and seniority of the people doing the work.

**Step 3: Set monitoring by tier.** Direct competitors monthly (case studies, job postings, client lists). Larger rivals quarterly (published thinking, public wins). Emerging threats quarterly.

**Step 4: Run win/loss conversations.** Talk to the buyers you won and lost. Record why, in their words.

**Step 5: Turn findings into a monthly one-page brief.** What changed, what it means for us, what we do about it. Send it to the owner and the sales lead.

## Layer 3: Force Multipliers

**Multiplier 1: Constraint mapping.** For each larger rival, list the minimum budget they can profitably serve and the staffing they cannot afford to offer. The segment below that line is where they cannot compete on unit economics.

**Multiplier 2: Build positioning on a repeated complaint.** If departing clients of a rival keep saying the same thing, such as being sold senior talent and given junior execution, turn the reverse into a guarantee you can keep.

**Multiplier 3: Battlecards for your top two rivals.** Their structural limits, their usual pitch and your answer. Refresh whenever you lose a deal to them.

**Multiplier 4: The one-sentence test.** Every team member states your differentiation in one sentence without preparing.

## Layer 4: Success Metrics

**Positive indicators:**
- Win rate against your main larger rivals in competitive proposals rises (a target above 40% is a workable start)
- Everyone on the team passes the one-sentence test
- Few competitive surprises per quarter
- The monthly brief is read and leads to at least one decision

**Failure signals:**
- A rival launches something you did not see coming (a monitoring gap)
- A larger rival opens a division aimed at your segment and you learn it from a client
- Your tracking sheet is a feature checklist
- The analysis has a completion date
- You spend more hours on rival websites than in buyer conversations

## Layer 5: Implementation Guidance

**Assign one owner per top competitor.** Give each a weekly checklist: site changes, recent content, hiring signals. Keep it to fifteen minutes.

**Build the first two battlecards before anything else.** They deliver value on the first sales call that uses them.

**Review quarterly.** Redraw the positioning map and check whether the gap you chose still exists.

**Common mistake: copying a strategy without its foundation.** A rival's approach works because of its customers, cost structure and distribution. Understand why before you adopt it.

**Edge case: two rivals move up-market together.** The pressure on your mid-market position changes even if neither targets you. Redraw the map.

**Edge case: a new entrant with a much lower price.** Check whether it serves your buyers at all before reacting. Move on price only if your positioning cannot answer it.
