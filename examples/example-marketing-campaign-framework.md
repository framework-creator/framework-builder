# Marketing Campaign Framework

*This framework was generated using the Framework Builder methodology. It demonstrates the five-layer DNA architecture applied to marketing campaigns for a local service business.*

Page: https://whereframeworks.com/frameworks/marketing-campaign
Guide: https://howtoframework.com/marketing-campaign-framework

## Layer 1: Principles Foundation

**Principle 1: Pick the conversion event that matches the buyer.** For a local service with urgent needs, a phone call converts at many times the rate of a form. Optimize every campaign for the call.

**Principle 2: The service area is the targeting constraint.** Paying for clicks outside the area you serve is waste. Geographic precision matters more than audience precision.

**Principle 3: Real demand cycles set the timing.** Plan around the seasons your customers actually need you, not the marketing calendar.

**Principle 4: Test the message before the design.** An ugly ad with the right message beats a beautiful ad with the wrong one.

**Principle 5: Every campaign has a hypothesis.** "Homeowners will respond to upfront pricing more than speed." Without one you cannot learn from the result.

## Layer 2: Systematic Approach

**Step 1: Route by urgency.** Emergency demand and planned maintenance are different campaigns.

**Step 2: Emergency path.** Build the emergency keyword set, write ads that match the urgency, put the phone number above the fold on a same-day landing page, turn on call tracking and launch.

**Step 3: Maintenance path.** Target homeowners in the service area on social and display channels. Use a longer window, a lead magnet such as a free inspection checklist and an email or text follow-up.

**Step 4: Start on one channel.** Prove the message and offer there before expanding.

**Step 5: Run a post-mortem.** Record which keywords drove calls, which copy cost least per call and which page converted best.

## Layer 3: Force Multipliers

**Multiplier 1: One message, many formats.** Pick the core promise, for example licensed, same-day, upfront pricing, and repeat it unchanged across ads, the business listing, the homepage and the vehicles. The format changes, the message stays.

**Multiplier 2: Small pre-launch tests.** Run each new variation for a small budget, such as $50 over three days, and compare call volume to your baseline before committing the monthly spend.

**Multiplier 3: A creative fatigue check.** After thirty days, compare week four with week one.

**Multiplier 4: A playbook from the first three months.** It becomes the template for the next channel.

**Multiplier 5: Pair each campaign with the same landing promise.** The page a click lands on repeats the ad's exact promise and shows the phone number first. A mismatch between ad and page wastes the click you paid for.

## Layer 4: Success Metrics

**Positive indicators:**
- Cost per call by channel under target (for example, under $35 for emergency, under $20 for maintenance)
- Call-to-appointment rate above 60%
- Return of roughly 5:1 on campaign spend
- Each campaign report names its hypothesis and the result

**Failure signals:**
- Reports lead with impressions, likes or followers
- Cost per call rises more than 25% between week one and week four of the same ad
- Budget is split across several channels before one has a baseline
- No one can say what the last campaign was meant to prove
- Post-mortems are skipped

**Rotate creative on evidence.** Compare the cost per call of any ad running for thirty days against its first week. Replace it when the cost has climbed more than 25%, and keep the old version in the playbook.

## Layer 5: Implementation Guidance

**Begin with emergency service on one search channel.** It has the highest intent and the quickest feedback.

**Document the first three months.** Then expand to maintenance campaigns using the same structure with different routing.

**Review quarterly.** Compare this quarter's cost per acquisition to last quarter's.

**Common mistake: perfecting creative before validating the message.**

**Edge case: call volume is high and appointments are low.** The problem is in call handling or scheduling, not the ad. Check answer speed and scripts.

**Edge case: a competitor floods your keywords in peak season.** Shift budget to the maintenance path and to listings and referrals until their spend drops.

**Common mistake: running campaigns with no hypothesis.** Without something testable, such as "homeowners will respond more to upfront pricing than to speed," you cannot learn from a result.

**Common mistake: vanity metrics.** A campaign with 100,000 impressions and no customers has failed. Anchor every report to cost per acquisition and return on spend. The rest is context.

**Common mistake: launching on several channels at once.** Spreading budget before one channel is proven is how budgets disappear. Get a baseline cost per acquisition first, then expand and compare.

**Common mistake: skipping post-mortems.** Without them each campaign team repeats the last team's mistakes, such as not knowing video beat static images last quarter. A post-mortem turns one result into organizational memory.

**Edge case: a seasonal spike you did not plan for.** Pause the maintenance path, move budget to the emergency path and keep call tracking on, then log what the spike taught you for next season's plan.
