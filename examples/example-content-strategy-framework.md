# Content Strategy Framework

*This framework was generated using the Framework Builder methodology. It demonstrates the five-layer DNA architecture applied to content strategy for a B2B company.*

Page: https://whereframeworks.com/frameworks/content-strategy
Guide: https://howtoframework.com/content-strategy-framework

---

## Layer 1: Principles Foundation

**Principle 1: Every piece answers a question a buyer asks.** If you cannot name the question, do not publish the piece.

**Principle 2: Depth beats frequency.** One research-backed guide a month builds more authority than several shallow posts a week, especially in long sales cycles.

**Principle 3: The sales team is a distribution channel.** Each piece should be something a salesperson can send with a note that says it answers what was discussed.

**Principle 4: Content is an asset, not a campaign.** Assets compound. A guide to a persistent problem keeps earning. A launch post expires.

**Principle 5: Write for the reader first.** Content built to please an algorithm may rank and still convert at nearly nothing.

## Layer 2: Systematic Approach

**Step 1: Audit what exists.** Count the pieces, find the few that drive most traffic and the many that get almost none.

**Step 2: Map the audience.** Name the buyer personas, for example the technical evaluator, the business decision-maker and the end user, and what each needs to see.

**Step 3: Build topic clusters around real objections.** List the five questions sales hears most. Each becomes a cluster with one pillar page and five to seven supporting pieces matched to persona and awareness stage.

**Step 4: Sequence production.** Start with the cluster tied to the most common objection.

**Step 5: Update before you create.** Refresh the top performers with links and calls to action before writing anything new.

## Layer 3: Force Multipliers

**Multiplier 1: Repurpose each pillar into five formats.** A downloadable guide for the business reader, technical documentation for the evaluator, a recorded session for the user, short social posts for reach and a one-pager for the sales team.

**Multiplier 2: A central resource hub.** Link every cluster to it, so it becomes the strongest page on the site.

**Multiplier 3: Publish data only you have.** Benchmarks from your own customer base are a moat a rival cannot copy.

**Multiplier 4: Refresh on a schedule.** Updating your top twenty pieces often returns more than twenty new ones.

## Layer 4: Success Metrics

**Positive indicators:**
- Weekly: organic traffic by cluster, new rankings in target topics, list growth from downloads
- Monthly: pipeline influenced by content, and the share of salespeople who send it
- Quarterly: revenue attributed to content, and acquisition cost for content-sourced leads against paid leads
- Authority in each topic cluster rises

**Failure signals:**
- Publishing volume is the headline number
- Salespeople never send the content
- Traffic grows and conversions do not
- Top pieces slip down the rankings and nobody notices
- New posts do not link to any cluster

## Layer 5: Implementation Guidance

**Month one:** refresh the top twelve posts with links and calls to action, and build the first cluster.

**Months two and three:** publish the pillar page and five supporting pieces, and run the repurposing chain for each.

**Month four:** review which channels performed, adjust the distribution plan and begin cluster two. By month six, expect three clusters and two full feedback cycles.

**Common mistake: scattered publishing.** Random posts start from zero each time. Clusters compound.

**Edge case: no sales team to ask.** Use support tickets, sales-call recordings or buyer interviews to list the real questions.

**Edge case: a topic with little search volume.** If buyers ask it during evaluation, build it anyway and distribute it directly.
